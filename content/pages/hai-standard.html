HAI Standard

AI that answers to people.

The HAI Standard sets out what any organization that builds or deploys AI owes the people it affects. It has nine pillars, and an organization answers to all nine. IRIS uses it to read the AI in the products you use.

AI already outpaces people at speed, scale and pattern recognition. Moral judgment in context, real relationships and accountability still need a person who can answer for the result. A system with people overseeing it fails gently and can be corrected. The HAI Standard writes that oversight into how AI is built.

The nine pillars

Version 1.1

I

Integrity & Transparency

State the purpose plainly, explain decisions, use no dark patterns.

II

Accountability Chain

A named person who can be reached is responsible before anything ships.

III

Safety & Robustness

Test under attack and across the whole range of people who'll use it. Keep watching after launch.

IV

Equality & Non-Discrimination

Test for unfair outcomes all the time, and correct them when they're found.

V

Human Override & Control

A person can review and reverse any decision that matters.

VI

Use Limits & Proportionality

AI is used only for the purposes it was disclosed for. New uses need consent.

VII

Data Ownership & Empowerment

People can see, correct, limit and delete their data in practice, beyond what the policy says.

VIII

Independent Audit

Auditors with no financial tie to the company, and findings published in full.

IX

Human Dignity Across the Full Stack

Stronger protection for vulnerable people, and disclosure of the labour and supply chain behind the AI.

How it's checked

Three levels of evidence.

Each pillar can be checked at three levels: Declared, from what the organization publishes; Verified, with active testing and documents; Certified, where a qualified independent reviewer checks the pillars that need a person's judgment. The coverage is public. The scoring weights stay private, so they can't be gamed.

In IRIS

The standard, applied where you are.

When IRIS reads a public code repository, it scores what the software does with people's data against the nine pillars, with any machine learning and its model card. OMARO holds its own products to the same standard and publishes the results on the Transparency page.
