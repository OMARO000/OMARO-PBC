Origins

The thinking behind the work.

OMARO began with one question: who holds the authority when a person uses technology? Four papers set out the answer and the standards that follow from it.

The white papers

Four papers.

In revision

The OMARO White Paper

Why OMARO exists, how a public benefit corporation keeps its purpose, and what it builds. It starts from how people hand authority over their lives to systems they never chose, and ends with what becomes possible when tools hand it back.

In writing

The IRIS White Paper

What a co-operating system is and how IRIS works: reading policies on the device, what each layer checks, how accuracy is measured in public, and how IRIS is built and audited.

In revision

The HAI Standard White Paper

Nine pillars for AI that answers to people, from transparency and a named chain of accountability to data ownership, independent audit and the dignity of everyone whose work makes AI possible. An executive edition follows it.

In writing

The HAI Rubric

How the HAI Standard is scored: the evidence each pillar asks for at the Declared, Verified and Certified levels. It's versioned on its own, so the standard can stay stable while the scoring improves.

Each paper will be posted here as a PDF with its SHA-256, so a downloaded copy can be checked.

How IRIS is built

Written with AI, directed by a person.

IRIS is written with AI and directed by its founder, who decides what it does and tests every build. Every change is tested, the methods are published, and outside experts check the work before anyone pays for it.
